Case Study — Accessibility & Service Design

Explorer Elite
The Curb-to-Gate Companion for Elderly

Client Massport (Boston Logan)
Team Anna, Trong, Maria, Kells
Focus Elderly Travelers
Tools Figma, FigJam, Miro
Tags
Service Mapping Accessibility UX Research Wayfinding

01 — Overview

Making every step of the airport journey feel youthful and independent again

Boston Logan Airport’s curb-to-gate experience is stressful for many travelers — especially elderly passengers facing mobility challenges, language barriers, and confusing navigation.

Explorer Elite (part of the WiseWanderers project) is a companion app and service ecosystem designed to reduce friction, reward loyalty, and turn waiting time into delight for older adults.

85% of elderly users find it difficult to locate their gate
70% require assistance or a travel partner
61% prefer shorter lines to enjoy time at the airport

02 — Research Findings

Understanding the real barriers

Through persona development, empathy mapping, and journey mapping, we surfaced key pain points for elderly travelers like Mrs. Nhung Trương.

“Why are they yelling? Where is my gate? Am I going the right way?”

Key Needs

  • Safety & Reduced Physical Strain — Fear of falling, difficulty standing in long lines, limited mobility.
  • Language & Cognitive Support — Limited English, dyslexia, vision impairment, too many signs.
  • Independence — Ability to navigate without constant help from family or staff.
  • Delight & Dignity — Turn waiting into enjoyable time with rewards, quiet zones, and easy assistance requests.

03 — Process

From research to high-fidelity prototype

We followed a human-centered design process including empathy mapping, pain point analysis, service blueprinting, journey mapping, card sorting, and iterative wireframing & testing.

1

Persona & Empathy Mapping

Developed primary persona Mrs. Nhung Trương (72, Vietnamese-American, post-fall mobility issues, limited English).

2

Service & Journey Mapping

Created detailed end-to-end blueprint from booking to boarding, identifying frontstage/backstage actions and opportunities.

3

Card Sorting & Sitemap

Organized features into intuitive navigation: Flight, Rewards, Amenities, Map, Help.

4

Wireframing & Hi-Fi Design

Low-fidelity to high-fidelity prototypes with large text, high contrast, simple flows, and AR navigation support.

04 — Solution

Explorer Elite — Key Features

  • One-tap Assistance Requests — Wheelchair, luggage help, language support — sent directly to airport staff.
  • AR + Floor-level Wayfinding — Turn-by-turn navigation with large, clear visuals and voice guidance.
  • Rewards & Points System — Earn points for checking in, requesting help, or leaving reviews — redeem for discounts at airport cafes/shops.
  • Personalized Boarding Alerts & Quiet Zones — Never miss a boarding call; find accessible seating easily.
  • Multilingual Support — Vietnamese-first interface with translation tools.

05 — Final Design

Seamless curb-to-gate experience

The high-fidelity prototype includes a clean homescreen with flight status, quick-access help, rewards, dining, and map. Users can request assistance, navigate with AR, and enjoy personalized offers.

06 — Reflections

What we learned

  • Simplicity and clarity are non-negotiable for low-tech and vision-impaired users — large text, high contrast, and minimal clicks win.
  • Pre-requesting assistance dramatically reduces anxiety compared to asking on-site.
  • Rewards and small delights (discounts, quiet spots) turn a stressful experience into a joyful one.
  • Service design must bridge digital and physical — the app works best when tightly integrated with airport staff and infrastructure.
  • Designing for accessibility benefits everyone.
